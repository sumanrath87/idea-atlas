## People and documents

**IDEA.** The Individuals with Disabilities Education Act, the federal law that guarantees eligible children with disabilities a free appropriate public education. Part B covers ages 3 through 21. Part C covers infants and toddlers.

**IEP (Individualized Education Program).** A written plan for a student who qualifies for special education. It lists the student's present levels, annual goals, the services and supports the school will provide, and how progress will be measured.

**IEP team.** The group that writes the IEP. It includes the parents, at least one general education teacher (if the child is or may be in general classes), at least one special education teacher, a district representative, someone who can explain evaluation results, and, when appropriate, the student.

**Section 504 plan.** A plan under Section 504 of the Rehabilitation Act, a civil rights law, that gives a student with a disability accommodations so they can take part in school. A student can have a 504 plan without qualifying for special education under IDEA.

**Parent Training and Information Center.** A federally funded organization in every state that helps families of children with disabilities understand their rights and take part in decisions, at no cost.

## Services and settings

**FAPE (free appropriate public education).** Special education and related services, at no cost to families, that meet state standards and follow the student's IEP.

**Related services.** Supports a student needs to benefit from special education, such as speech-language therapy, occupational therapy, counseling, or transportation.

**Supplementary aids and services.** Supports that help a student learn in general classes, such as a co-teacher, adapted materials, or assistive technology.

**Least restrictive environment (LRE).** IDEA's rule that students with disabilities learn with students without disabilities to the maximum extent appropriate. See the [guide to LRE]({base}guides/least-restrictive-environment/).

**Continuum of placements.** The range of settings every district must have available, from general classes to special classes, special schools, and home or hospital instruction.

**Transition services.** Planned activities that help a student move from school to life after school, such as further education, work, and living on their own. Required in the IEP by age 16.

## Evaluation and rights

**Child find.** A district's duty to find, evaluate, and identify every child with a disability who may need special education, including children in private schools and children who are homeless.

**Evaluation.** The assessments a team uses to decide whether a child has a disability and needs special education. The initial evaluation must happen within 60 days of parent consent, or the state's own timeline.

**Independent educational evaluation (IEE).** An evaluation by someone outside the school. Parents who disagree with the school's evaluation can ask for one at public expense.

**Prior written notice.** A written explanation the school must give when it proposes or refuses to change a child's identification, evaluation, placement, or services.

**Procedural safeguards.** The rights IDEA gives parents, such as consent, notice, access to records, and the ways to settle disagreements. Schools must give parents a written summary at least once a year.

**Manifestation determination.** A review, within 10 school days of deciding to change a student's placement for discipline, of whether the behavior was caused by the disability or by a failure to carry out the IEP.

## Settling disagreements

**State complaint.** A written complaint to the state education agency that a school or district broke an IDEA rule. The state must decide within 60 days.

**Mediation.** A voluntary, confidential meeting with a neutral mediator to reach an agreement. Free to families.

**Due process complaint.** A request for a hearing before an impartial hearing officer about a child's identification, evaluation, placement, or education.

**Resolution meeting.** A meeting the district must hold within 15 days after a parent files a due process complaint, to try to settle it before a hearing.

## Data and reporting

**Child count.** The number of children receiving special education under IDEA, reported each fall by districts and states.

**Civil Rights Data Collection (CRDC).** A survey of nearly every public school by the U.S. Department of Education's Office for Civil Rights. The source of the atlas's discipline, restraint, seclusion, and Section 504 figures.

**Physical restraint.** Holding a student so they cannot move freely. **Mechanical restraint** uses a device or equipment to restrict movement. **Seclusion** is confining a student alone in a room or area they are physically prevented from leaving.

**Per 100.** A rate that tells you how many of every 100 students in a group had something happen. It lets you compare small and large districts.

**Median (typical district).** The district in the middle when all districts in a state are lined up from lowest to highest.

**Middle half.** The range where the middle 50% of districts fall, between the 25th and 75th percentiles.

**SPP/APR.** The State Performance Plan and Annual Performance Report, in which each state reports on 18 federal indicators. See [the federal indicators]({base}guides/indicators/).

**FFY (federal fiscal year).** The year label used in federal reports. FFY 2024 data generally describe the 2024–25 school year.

**State determination.** The U.S. Department of Education's yearly rating of each state: meets requirements, needs assistance, needs intervention, or needs substantial intervention.

**Significant disproportionality.** A finding that students of one race or ethnicity are identified, placed, or disciplined at markedly higher rates than others in a district. See [the guide]({base}guides/disproportionality/).

**OSEP.** The Office of Special Education Programs, the part of the U.S. Department of Education that oversees IDEA.
