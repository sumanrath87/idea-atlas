## The reports

Since the 2004 reauthorization of IDEA, each state has written a State Performance Plan and reported on it every year in an Annual Performance Report, together called the SPP/APR. States submit it each February. The U.S. Department of Education reviews it and uses it, with other information, to give each state its yearly rating: meets requirements, needs assistance, needs intervention, or needs substantial intervention. Each [state page]({base}states/) in the atlas shows the 2026 rating.

States must also report publicly on how each district did against the state's targets on indicators 1 through 14, within 120 days of submitting the SPP/APR (34 CFR 300.602). Your state education agency's website is the place to look for your district's results.

## Results and compliance

The indicators come in two kinds.

| Kind | What it asks | Target | Indicators |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Results** | How are students doing? | Set by each state | 1, 2, 3, 4A, 5, 6, 7, 8, 14, 15, 16, 17 |
| **Compliance** | Did required steps happen, on time and in full? | 100% (or 0% for counts of problems) | 4B, 9, 10, 11, 12, 13, 18 |

For years, federal ratings of states rested on the compliance indicators alone. In 2014 the U.S. Department of Education added results, under an approach it calls Results Driven Accountability. The essay [Beyond compliance]({base}guides/beyond-compliance/) looks at why that shift matters.

## The year labels

Federal reports label data by federal fiscal year (FFY). The FFY 2024 report, submitted in February 2026, mostly describes the 2024–25 school year. A few indicators lag a year: graduation, dropout, and discipline in the FFY 2024 report describe 2023–24. The atlas always prints the school year next to a figure.

## Which indicators the atlas shows

- **State pages** show indicators 7, 8, 11, 12, 13, and 14 for each state, with the national figure, plus the dispute resolution counts behind indicators 15 and 16.
- **District pages** show district figures that match indicators 1, 2, and 5, and related civil rights data for indicator 4.
- The atlas does not show indicators 3, 6, 17, or 18. Your state's SPP/APR, published on the U.S. Department of Education's IDEA website, includes them all.

## Beyond compliance

An indicator can tell you whether something happened. It cannot tell you whether it helped. Each indicator page ends with a few questions that go past the measure, drawing on Thomas Skrtic's work on how schools respond to mandates and Elizabeth Kozleski's work on change that involves the whole school community.
