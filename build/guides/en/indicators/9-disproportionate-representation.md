## What it measures

The percent of districts with disproportionate representation of racial and ethnic groups in special education and related services that is the result of inappropriate identification.

**Type:** Compliance indicator.

## How it is measured

States first use data to find districts with disproportionate representation, using their own method and thresholds. They then review those districts' policies, practices, and procedures to decide whether the representation is the result of inappropriate identification. Only districts where it is count. It is a compliance indicator, so the target is 0%.

## Where to find it

District pages show students with IEPs per 100 enrolled by race and ethnicity, from the 2023–24 Civil Rights Data Collection. These are not the state's findings. See the [guide to significant disproportionality]({base}guides/disproportionality/).

## Questions to ask

- How many districts did our state identify, and how did it decide whether identification was inappropriate?
- What does our district do before referring a student for evaluation?

## Beyond compliance

A finding under this indicator requires two steps: a data flag, then a review that concludes identification was inappropriate. Much depends on how searching that review is. McCall and Skrtic (2009) describe disproportionality as a "wicked problem" that does not yield to a single technical fix.

- What do teachers try in the general class before referring a student?
- Who looks at referral patterns across classrooms, and how often?
