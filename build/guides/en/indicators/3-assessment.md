## What it measures

Four measures, each for reading and math in grades 4, 8, and high school: (A) the share of students with IEPs who took the state test; (B) the share who were proficient against grade-level standards; (C) the share who were proficient against alternate standards, for students taking the alternate assessment; and (D) the gap in grade-level proficiency between students with IEPs and all students.

**Type:** Results indicator.

## How it is measured

States report from their own state tests, so proficiency rates cannot be compared from one state to another. Federal law expects nearly all students to take part, and limits the share of students who take the alternate assessment. Targets are set by each state.

## Where to find it

The atlas does not show test results. Your state education agency publishes them by district, usually with a separate figure for students with IEPs.

## Questions to ask

- Which tests does my child take, and with what accommodations?
- How large is the proficiency gap in our district, and is it shrinking?

## Beyond compliance

A proficiency rate measures one day's test against one standard. The Supreme Court has said an IEP must be appropriately ambitious in light of the child's circumstances (*Endrew F.*, 2017). That is a question about each child's growth, which a group rate cannot answer.

- Are students with IEPs taught grade-level content, or a reduced version of it?
- How do teachers know, during the year, whether a student is making the progress the IEP promised?
