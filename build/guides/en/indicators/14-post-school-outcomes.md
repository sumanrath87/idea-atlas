## What it measures

The percent of youth who had IEPs when they left high school and who, within one year of leaving, were (A) enrolled in higher education; (B) enrolled in higher education or competitively employed; or (C) enrolled in higher education or some other postsecondary education or training program, or competitively employed or in some other employment.

**Type:** Results indicator.

## How it is measured

States contact young people about a year after they leave school, usually by survey or interview. They must report how many responded and whether respondents reflect all leavers. The three measures build on each other, so C is always at least as high as A and B.

## Where to find it

On your [state's page]({base}states/), under "Results on federal indicators." The atlas shows the figures for young people who left high school in 2021–22.

## Questions to ask

- Does our district hear back from its graduates, and what does it learn?
- Which supports after high school are students connected with before they leave?

## Beyond compliance

Indicator 14 is one of the few federal measures that asks about life after school. Even so, it asks only whether a young person is in school or working. Kozleski and Thorius (2014) argue for centering students' own voices in school improvement.

- Are former students asked what they wish school had done differently?
- Do current students hear from graduates with disabilities about what came next?
