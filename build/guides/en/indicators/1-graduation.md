## What it measures

The percent of youth with IEPs, ages 14 through 21, who left special education by graduating with a regular high school diploma.

**Type:** Results indicator.

## How it is measured

States use the same federal exiting data the atlas shows under "Leaving school." The base is every student with an IEP who left school in one of five ways: a regular diploma, an alternate diploma, a certificate, reaching the maximum age for services, or dropping out. Students who moved away or transferred are not counted. Each state sets its own targets. The data lag a year: the FFY 2024 report uses students who left in 2023–24.

## Where to find it

District pages show the same measure for each district, in the "Leaving school" section. Your state's figure is in its yearly report.

## Questions to ask

- How does our district's diploma rate compare with the typical district in the state?
- What share of students leave with an alternate diploma or certificate instead, and how is that decided?

## Beyond compliance

A diploma rate counts who crossed a line, not what they were ready for. Skrtic (1991b) argued that schools serve students best when teachers solve problems together rather than sorting students into separate tracks.

- Were students with IEPs in courses that led to a regular diploma from the start, or moved off that path early?
- What do graduates themselves say prepared them, or did not?
