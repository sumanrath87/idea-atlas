## What it measures

The percent of youth with IEPs, ages 14 through 21, who left special education by dropping out.

**Type:** Results indicator.

## How it is measured

It uses the same base as indicator 1: every student with an IEP who left school by graduating, receiving an alternate diploma or certificate, reaching the maximum age, or dropping out. Under the federal definition, students who moved and are not known to be continuing in school are counted as dropouts. Each state sets its own targets, and the data lag a year.

## Where to find it

District pages show the district's rate in the "Leaving school" section.

## Questions to ask

- How many students with IEPs dropped out last year, and at what grades?
- What does the district do when a student with an IEP starts missing school?

## Beyond compliance

Dropping out is usually the end of a long process that a yearly rate cannot show. Kozleski and Thorius (2014) argue for placing students and families at the center of school improvement.

- Has anyone asked students who left why they left?
- Which school practices, such as long removals or schedules that do not work for a student, came before the decision to leave?
