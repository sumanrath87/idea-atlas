## What it measures

The percent of youth with IEPs aged 16 and above whose IEP includes appropriate, measurable postsecondary goals updated every year and based on an age-appropriate transition assessment; transition services, including courses of study, to help reach those goals; and annual IEP goals related to the student's transition needs. There must also be evidence that the student was invited to the IEP meeting where transition was discussed, and, with consent, that a representative of any likely adult agency was invited.

**Type:** Compliance indicator.

## How it is measured

States review a sample of IEPs against a checklist. An IEP missing any required part does not count. The target is 100%.

## Where to find it

On your [state's page]({base}states/), under "Results on federal indicators." The atlas shows the 2022–23 figure.

## Questions to ask

- What are my child's goals for after high school, in their own words?
- Which courses and work experiences in the IEP lead toward them?

## Beyond compliance

A transition plan can meet every item on the checklist and still be written for the student rather than with them. Skrtic (2005) named IEPs as an example of compliance that can stay separate from practice.

- Did the student lead or speak at the meeting?
- Is the plan revisited during the year, or only when the IEP is due?
