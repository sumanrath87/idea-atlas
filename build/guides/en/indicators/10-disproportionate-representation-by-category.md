## What it measures

The percent of districts with disproportionate representation of racial and ethnic groups in specific disability categories that is the result of inappropriate identification.

**Type:** Compliance indicator.

## How it is measured

It works like indicator 9, but for six categories: intellectual disability, specific learning disability, emotional disturbance, speech or language impairment, other health impairment, and autism. States use their own thresholds, then review policies and practices. The target is 0%.

## Where to find it

District pages show students with IEPs by federal category, and by race and ethnicity. See the [guide to significant disproportionality]({base}guides/disproportionality/).

## Questions to ask

- Which categories, if any, has our state flagged in our district?
- How are evaluation teams trained to tell a disability apart from a language difference or a gap in prior teaching?

## Beyond compliance

Categories such as emotional disturbance rest heavily on adults' judgments about behavior. Kozleski and Smith (2009) found that a school community's narratives about children shape how it responds to them.

- Whose observations lead to a referral in each category?
- Do families get to describe their child in their own words during evaluation?
