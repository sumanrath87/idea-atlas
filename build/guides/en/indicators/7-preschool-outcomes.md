## What it measures

For preschool children with IEPs who received services for at least six months, progress in three areas: (A) positive social-emotional skills, including relationships; (B) acquiring and using knowledge and skills, including early language and literacy; and (C) using appropriate behaviors to meet their own needs.

**Type:** Results indicator.

## How it is measured

Each area has two summary statements. The first is the share of children who entered below age expectations and grew substantially faster by the time they left. The second is the share functioning at age expectations when they left preschool. States choose their own tools for rating children and set their own targets.

## Where to find it

On your [state's page]({base}states/), under "Results on federal indicators." The atlas shows the second summary statement: children at age expectations when they left preschool, for the 2022–23 school year.

## Questions to ask

- How does the district measure my child's progress in these areas?
- How are families involved in deciding whether a child is at age expectations?

## Beyond compliance

These ratings depend on how each program observes children. Kozleski and Smith (2009) found that the stories adults tell about children shape what schools see in them.

- Do families' observations at home count in the rating?
- What changes in the classroom when a child is not making the expected progress?
