## What it measures

The percent of children with IEPs ages 3, 4, and 5 who are enrolled in a preschool program and (A) receive most of their special education and related services in a regular early childhood program, (B) attend a separate special education class, separate school, or residential facility, or (C) receive services in the home.

**Type:** Results indicator.

## How it is measured

States use the fall child count of early childhood environments. Five-year-olds in kindergarten are counted in indicator 5 instead. Each state sets targets.

## Where to find it

The atlas does not show preschool settings by district. Your state's report includes the statewide figure.

## Questions to ask

- Does our district offer preschool classes that include children without disabilities?
- If not, how does it serve preschoolers with IEPs alongside other children?

## Beyond compliance

Many districts do not run their own preschool for all children, which limits inclusive options before services begin. Kozleski and Thorius (2014) describe change that has to involve families and community partners, not only schools.

- Which community preschools partner with the district, and how are families told about them?
- Do families of preschoolers with IEPs have a real choice of setting?
