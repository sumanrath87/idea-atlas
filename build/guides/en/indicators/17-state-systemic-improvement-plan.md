## What it measures

Each state's multi-year State Systemic Improvement Plan (SSIP). The state chooses one measurable result for children with disabilities, called the State-identified Measurable Result, often early literacy or graduation, and plans how to improve it with input from families, educators, and others.

**Type:** Results indicator.

## How it is measured

States report each year on what they did, how they evaluated it, and progress on their chosen result. Targets are set by each state.

## Where to find it

The atlas does not show SSIP results. Your state education agency publishes its plan and yearly updates.

## Questions to ask

- Which result did our state choose, and why?
- Which districts take part, and how are families involved?

## Beyond compliance

The SSIP was added when federal oversight shifted from compliance toward results in 2014. Ferguson, Kozleski, and Smith (2001) describe lasting change as happening at several levels at once: classroom, school, district, and state.

- Do teachers in our district know what the state's plan is?
- Does the plan change classroom practice, or mostly produce reports?
